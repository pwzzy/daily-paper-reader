## ABSTRACT 

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong potential to automate heuristic design for NP-hard combinatorial optimization (CO) tasks without human intervention. Recent methods predominantly rely on evolutionary computation (EC) frameworks, where heuristic diversification is mainly induced through EC-like operators or downstream selection, while the direction of heuristic transforma-tion remains largely unharnessed. In this paper, we propose a multi-directional 

Perturbation– Rewrite Search ( PRS ) framework for automatic heuristic design that factorizes heuristic transformation through directional search primitives at the generation level. Specifically, PRS operates on heuristic ideas through three complementary directions: expansion toward richer and more expressive designs, compression toward simpler and less specialized designs, and steering for fine-grained refinement, together enabling broader and more sustained exploration of diverse regions in the heuristic design space. PRS further organizes candidate exploration within a breadth-first search-based process with adaptive operator control, which ensures thorough candidate utilization and sustained exploration, and maintains strong search dynamics while preventing premature convergence. Extensive empirical results across diverse CO tasks show that PRS consistently discovers high-quality heuristics, demonstrating the effectiveness and generality of explicitly directional exploration design. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Automatic heuristic design (AHD) aims to automatically discover high-performing heuristic algo-rithms for a wide range of optimization problems (He et al., 2011; Dahmani et al., 2024; Novikov et al., 2025). Despite its importance, traditional AHD methods, such as genetic programming, remain fundamentally constrained by their reliance on manually designed, problem-specific operators, which limits scalability and restricts the exploration of the heuristic search space. The recent emergence of large language models (LLMs) has opened a promising new direction for AHD (Liu et al., 2024b), enabling automated heuristic generation through LLM-based code synthesis and reasoning. Most existing LLM-based AHD approaches build upon optimization frameworks (e.g., evolutionary computation (EC)), where heuristics are iteratively improved using LLMs (Romera-Paredes et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024a; Ye et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2025; Shi et al., 2026). Maintaining diverse heuristic candidates is important for preventing premature concentration around a narrow family of designs and for preserving access to alternative regions that may become useful at different stages of search. Recent works have attempted to enhance the search process with more structured control, such as diversity-aware population management (Dat et al., 2025) and feature-assisted partitioning of the abstract heuristic search space (Hu & Zhang, 2025). For example, EvoX (Liu et al., 2026) adaptively evolves the search strategy that governs parent selection and candidate variation, dynamically switching among refinement, structural variation, and free-form variation to balance exploitation and diversification according to search progress. Despite these advances, existing approaches mainly encourage diversification through population management, candidate selection, similarity measures, or search-space partitioning. Such mechanisms regulate which candidates are retained, preferred, or grouped after or around generation, but they do 1arXiv:2610.31048v1 [cs.AI] 22 Oct 2026 Preprint not explicitly structure the generation process itself around different directions of heuristic change. As a result, heuristic diversification is typically an emergent consequence of generic mutation, crossover, reflection, or novelty pressure, rather than a directly controlled search primitive. This leaves an important gap: existing AHD frameworks lack explicit generation primitives for deliberately diversifying heuristic ideas along complementary semantic directions. In this work, we argue that diversification should be controlled directly at the level of heuristic generation. To this end, we propose Perturbation–Rewrite Search (PRS) , a multi-directional search framework that explicitly factorizes heuristic generation into complementary search primitives over the idea space. PRS introduces three perturbation strategies, i.e., expansion , compression ,and steering , which deliberately transform a parent heuristic toward richer, simpler, and locally refined designs, respectively. Rather than using diversity only as a population property or selection signal, PRS controls how new heuristic ideas are produced before they are realized as executable implementations. These abstraction-level perturbations are then instantiated through an LLM-based rewriting process that translates the modified heuristic specification into executable code while preserving its intended decision logic. Together, this primitive-level directional diversification enables broader and more sustained coverage of structural regions in the heuristic space. To further enhance search effectiveness, PRS organizes candidate exploration within a breadth-first search (BFS) framework. By expanding multiple candidates layer by layer, PRS improves candidate utilization while preventing early domination by a small subset of heuristics. This search strategy is coupled with an adaptive exploration control mechanism, enabling sustained exploration and robustness against stagnation. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We introduce Perturbation–Rewrite Search (PRS) for automatic heuristic design, which makes directional heuristic transformation an explicit generation-level search primitive for structured exploration of the heuristic idea space. • We develop a multi-directional perturbation mechanism with expansion, compression, and steering, which deliberately transforms heuristic ideas toward richer, simpler, and locally refined designs, respectively. PRS further integrates bounded BFS-based search and adaptive operator control to allocate search effort across these complementary directions. • We comprehensively evaluate PRS across 11 combinatorial optimization problem settings and show that it consistently discovers high-quality heuristics, demonstrating strong effec-tiveness and generality compared to state-of-the-art LLM-based AHD methods.

For example, in Travelling Salesman Problem (TSP), existing LLM-based AHD methods (Liu et al., 2024a) typically focus on designing step-by-step constructive heuristics that select the next node based on the current partial tour. Beyond constructive frameworks, recent studies have also explored learning heuristics that update weighted distance matrices within the broader Guided Local Search (GLS) algorithm, thereby more effectively guiding local search decisions (Ye et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2025). A prominent line of LLM-based AHD methods follows an evolutionary paradigm (Liu et al., 2023; Romera-Paredes et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024a; Ye et al., 2024). Using EC-style operators such as crossover and mutation, these methods progressively explore the heuristic space, with more recent extensions further strengthening this paradigm. For example, HiFo-Prompt (Chen et al., 2026) 2Preprint introduces hierarchical prompt-level guidance through hindsight–foresight control and knowledge distillation for faster and more efficient heuristic search. Despite differences in evolution strategies, these frameworks remain rooted in EC-like search, where search operators typically recombine multiple heuristics in crossover to inherit parental features, together with mutation-style operators to promote diversification. Consequently, the direction of heuristic change is largely uncontrolled by the search mechanism itself: different transformation patterns may emerge during evolution, but they are not explicitly harnessed as distinct search primi-tives. In contrast, PRS directly structures heuristic generation through complementary transformation directions, enabling multi-directional exploration without relying primarily on recombination of existing structures. 

Breadth First Search. Breadth-first search is a fundamental graph traversal strategy that explores states layer by layer by maintaining a frontier of candidate states at each depth. Recent work such as Tree-of-Thoughts (Yao et al., 2023) instantiates BFS in the space of intermediate reasoning trajectories, where each layer corresponds to a single reasoning step and the frontier is pruned to a fixed breadth. In a generic BFS procedure, the search starts from an initial state and expands all states in the current frontier using a predefined expansion operator. The resulting states are then scored, and a subset of top-ranked states is retained as the frontier for the next layer. This process repeats until a stopping criterion, such as a maximum search depth or a computational budget, is reached.

In this section, we introduce PRS, a multi-directional perturbation–rewrite search framework that makes directional heuristic transformation an explicit search primitive for automatic heuristic design. PRS explores the heuristic space by applying multi-directional perturbations to heuristic ideas and then rewriting the corresponding implementations to realize the modified heuristic as executable candidates. At the search level, PRS maintains a heuristic pool and organizes candidate expansion in a breadth-first manner with selective pruning to enhance candidate utilization. In the following subsections, we describe the perturbation and rewrite process, the BFS-based search structure, and the adaptive exploration controller in detail. 3.1 PERTURBATION -R EWRITE OPERATOR 

PRS introduces a multi-directional perturbation–rewrite process composed of a perturbation step followed by an LLM-based rewrite step. Rather than directly modifying executable heuristics through carefully crafted EC operators, PRS applies directional perturbations to heuristic ideas and subsequently rewrites the corresponding implementations to realize the modified heuristics as executable candidates. This design separates the intended algorithmic modification from its low-3Preprint level implementation details, enabling controlled changes in heuristic structure while preserving the executability and semantic coherence required for black-box heuristic evaluation. We denote hp as the parent heuristic used for perturbation–rewrite search, and elaborate the perturbation and rewriting strategies below. 

Multi-directional Perturbation. The goal of multi-directional perturbation is to explore the heuristic space through complementary transformation directions, thereby overcoming the localized perturba-tion nature of traditional mutation operators. This design is motivated by the fact that heuristic quality does not vary monotonically with structural complexity: overly simple heuristics may lack expressive power, whereas overly complex ones may become bulky, computationally expensive, or over-fit to in-distributional data. Accordingly, PRS employs three complementary perturbation actions that address different search needs: expansion increases heuristic expressiveness, compression counteracts excessive specialization, and steering provides refinement around promising heuristic structures. 

Expansion (Φ expn  

> θ

). The expansion operator explicitly prompts LLMs to enrich the parent heuristic by introducing additional decision signals, interactions, conditional rules, or adaptive mechanisms, deliberately increasing its structural complexity and expressive capacity. Specifically, expansion instructs LLMs to inject new structural components, such as additional scoring terms or conditional logic. The operator further specifies how these additions affect heuristic decisions rather than simply appending independent components. From an optimization perspective, expansion biases heuristic structures toward more elaborate and expressive programmatic forms, such as deeper control flow or richer operator compositions, thereby increasing its expressive capacity and conceptual richness. 

Compression (Φ comp  

> θ

). Complementary to expansion, PRS employs a compression operator that biases LLMs toward reducing the structural complexity of the current parent heuristic by removing redundant mechanisms or over-specialized components that may hinder out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization (e.g., structures tightly coupled to specific problem sizes or data distributions). Intu-itively, compression may disrupt the established advantages of the parent heuristic, as simplification can often lead to short-term performance degradation. However, from a broader perspective, it may mitigate the accumulation of dominant structural priors and excessive complexity, acting as a potential refactoring mechanism in the heuristic space that counteracts over-specialization and promotes more robust heuristic representations.

Steering complements these directional perturbations by performing localized refinements while approximately preserving the overall heuristic structure, allowing PRS to further exploit promising regions without inducing another substantial structural drift. Together, expansion, compression, and steering constitute a multi-directional perturbation mechanism that enables PRS to explore both richer and more generalized heuristics across complementary regimes. The BFS-based heuristic search with the perturbation–rewrite operator constitutes the core of PRS. In this subsection, we introduce an adaptive exploration controller that serves as an auxiliary mechanism to balance exploration and exploitation, and to mitigate search stagnation in local optima.

Excessive exploration can lead 5Preprint to random drift and insufficient refinement of promising elites, whereas overly conservative updates or exploitation may cause the search to collapse into a narrow region of the solution space. Stagnation-Aware Restart. While adaptive operator control improves the overall trade-off between exploration and exploitation, it cannot guarantee escape from stagnation in local optima (Dat et al., 2025). As the search progresses, repeatedly exploiting successful perturbation directions and promis-ing parents may gradually concentrate the search around a narrow set of heuristic structures, making further improvements increasingly difficult. This behavior arises because the best-performing heuris-tics can repeatedly attract search effort within their local neighborhoods, leading to the proliferation of structurally similar candidates. As a result, the search may enter a plateau regime, where heuristic diversity diminishes due to premature convergence or inbreeding (Eshelman, 1991). To address this issue, PRS triggers a stagnation-aware restart when no improvement in the global best heuristic is observed for a predefined number C of heuristic evaluations. Once activated, two coordinated actions are performed: a) Elite Restart , where the heuristic pool is reset to retain only the globally best heuristic discovered so far; and b) Operator Reheating , where the recent operator reward window is reset and the UCB exploration coefficient is temporarily restored to its initial value 

c0, shifting operator selection back toward exploration before gradually returning to reward-guided exploitation. Together, these mechanisms enable the heuristic search to escape the current plateau by reintroducing diversity into the heuristic pool while ensuring smooth convergence through restarting from the elite heuristic. Implementation Setups. To maximize the effectiveness of BFS, we adopt a beam search with beam size κ = 3 and maximum tree depth d = 3 , and set the frontier size (i.e., the maximum number of parent nodes for the next layer) to f s = 3 . ## 5 DISCUSSIONS 

Ablations on Mechanisms. We analyze the necessity of each component in PRS. As shown in Table 4, all three perturbation operators contribute to search effectiveness: expansion and compression support complementary transformation directions by encouraging richer and simpler heuristic designs, respectively, while steering provides fine-grained exploitation around promising heuristic structures. Disabling UCB-based operator selection and replacing it with random selection consistently degrades performance, indicating that adaptively allocating search effort among the three operators is beneficial. Disabling the stagnation-aware restart mechanism ( C = 10 5) also weakens performance, further showing the importance of adaptive search control for sustained exploration. ## 6 CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we propose PRS, a multi-directional search framework for automatic heuristic design that makes directional heuristic transformation an explicit generation-level search primitive. By organizing heuristic generation through complementary transformation directions and combining them with breadth-first search and adaptive operator control, PRS actively directs diversification, sustains exploration, and mitigates premature convergence. Extensive experiments across diverse combinatorial optimization tasks demonstrate that PRS consistently discovers high-quality heuristics and outperforms existing LLM-based AHD methods in various settings, emphasizing the importance of exploring directionally diverse regions of the heuristic search space.

## A RELATED WORK 

A.1 AUTOMATED HEURISTIC DESIGN 

Automated Heuristic Design (AHD), often referred to as hyper-heuristics (Burke et al., 2018), aims to automatically discover high-performing heuristics from a large space of valid heuristic candidates rather than directly solving problem instances. Most AHD methods adopt evolutionary algorithms as the underlying search strategy to iteratively refine heuristics automatically through selection and generation, as well as a series of defined evolutionary operators for combination and adaptation (Pillay & Qu, 2018). Among them, genetic programming (GP) (Langdon & Poli, 2013) has emerged as a prevailing approach for heuristic generation due to its flexibility and interpretability, enabling the synthesis of reusable or instance-specific heuristics via tree-based representations. However, GP-based AHD typically relies on carefully hand-crafted mutation and crossover operators, whose effectiveness is highly sensitive to representation choices and domain knowledge (Duflo et al., 2019). Beyond GP, recent AHD frameworks further categorize heuristic generation into constructive and perturbative paradigms, or integrate multiple operators and stages through component-based unified solvers, with the goal of enhancing generality across diverse optimization problems (Bezerra et al., 2015; Qu et al., 2020). Despite these advances, existing AHD approaches largely depend on manually designed components and expert knowledge (Liu et al., 2025), which remain a major bottleneck for scalability and portability. A.2 LLM-BASED AHD Recent work has introduced large language models into automated heuristic design, leading to a class of population-based LLM-driven AHD methods that largely follow an evolutionary paradigm (Liu et al., 2023; Romera-Paredes et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024a; Ye et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024c). In general, these methods iterate over three core steps after initially sampling a set of heuristic func-tions: selecting mutation or crossover operators together with parent heuristics from the population, prompting LLMs to generate new heuristic implementations—typically in executable code form—and updating the population (or the tree) based on empirical performance evaluated on a validation dataset until a fixed maximum evaluation step is reached. Representative frameworks within this paradigm include FunSearch (Romera-Paredes et al., 2024), which adopts a multi-island evolutionary structure with a fixed prompt strategy for algorithm refinement, EoH (Liu et al., 2024a), which expands the search space through multiple prompt strategies that evolve both intermediate reasoning and code, and ReEvo (Ye et al., 2024), which incorporates short- and long-term reflection mechanisms to guide heuristic evolution. More recently, diversity has received increasing attention as an important factor for sustaining exploration in LLM-based AHD. For example, HSEvo (Dat et al., 2025) explicitly promotes population diversity through diversity-aware search and population management, showing the value of preserving heterogeneous heuristic candidates during evolution. Building on this direction, recent studies have introduced more explicit mechanisms for measuring diversity and structuring the search space. BehaveSim (Zhang & Lu, 2026) measures algorithmic similarity from execution trajectories and incorporates behavioral diversity into candidate selec-tion. PartEvo (Hu & Zhang, 2025) projects candidate algorithms into feature spaces and constructs semantic niches to allocate search effort across different regions of the abstract language space. ShinkaEvolve (Lange et al., 2025) further promotes exploration through archive-based parent sam-pling and code-novelty rejection based on embedding similarity. These approaches demonstrate the importance of maintaining diverse candidates during LLM-based algorithm search, but primar-ily regulate diversity through similarity assessment, population organization, novelty filtering, or search-space partitioning. In contrast, PRS controls diversification directly at the generation level by explicitly selecting how a parent heuristic is transformed before realization, using expansion, compression, and steering as complementary directional perturbation operators. A.3 LLM FOR CO Beyond LLM-based AHD, the use of LLMs for tackling combinatorial optimization (CO) problems has also emerged as a promising direction, and one line of research treats LLMs as optimizers. Starting with OPRO (Yang et al., 2023) that iteratively refines candidate solutions for TSP by conditioning on previously generated solutions and their objective values, the existing LLM-as-15 Preprint optimizer methods (Guo et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024d; Elhenawy et al., 2024) improve solution quality through repeated in-context prompting, evaluation, and reinjection, forming a closed-loop optimization process. Another closely related direction employs LLMs as end-to-end solvers (LLM-as-E2E-solver) that directly map problem descriptions to complete solutions, either via prompting or with additional fine-tuning within task-specific environments (Abgaryan et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2025). These approaches demonstrate the potential of LLMs to reason over complicated CO structures and to generate feasible solutions without explicit algorithmic decomposition and solver utilization. A.4 ADAPTIVE CONTROL IN EC AND LLM-BASED AHD. Adapting search strategies based on the evolving state has long been a crucial objective in evolutionary computation. Traditional approaches mainly rely on low-level mechanisms such as Adaptive Operator Selection (AOS) (Fialho, 2010) and parameter control (Eiben & Smith, 2015), which adjust mutation rates or operator probabilities in a reactive manner. Such approaches operate through numerical credit assignment mechanisms that bias the search toward recently successful strategies, but they lack the capacity to reason about search dynamics, for example, detecting stagnation or loss of diversity. More recent LLM-based AHD methods have also introduced higher-level mechanisms for adapting search behavior, such as diversity-aware population management and stagnation handling (Dat et al., 2025). These developments motivate adaptive search control beyond fixed operator schedules. Driven by these motivations, PRS combines adaptive operator control with stagnation-aware restart, where operator selection is continuously adjusted according to recent search utility, while prolonged stagnation triggers an elite restart and temporary reheating of exploration.

Flow Shop Scheduling Problems (FSSP). Scheduling problems aim to determine the execution order of operations to optimize the overall schedule length. In the Flow Shop Scheduling Problem (FSSP), n jobs must be processed on m machines, where each job consists of m operations executed in the same predetermined order across all machines. Following prior work (Emmons & Vairaktarakis, 2012), we consider a perturbation-based scheduling setting, where the processing order remains consistent during each step, and the machines cannot execute multiple operations simultaneously. During heuristic search, we perform optimization on a set of randomly generated instances D. Each instance in D consists of 50 jobs and between 2 and 20 machines, with processing times sampled independently from a uniform distribution over [0 , 1] 

(Pan et al., 2021). The average makespan over these instances is used as the fitness value for heuristic evaluation.

Step-by-step construction is a generic framework for combinatorial optimization that incrementally builds a solution by repeatedly selecting and adding the highest-priority candidate until a complete feasible solution is obtained. Nearest-neighbor is a widely used handcrafted step-by-step constructive heuristic for TSP, which selects the next node solely based on its distance to the current node. Analogously, for the knapsack problem, a greedy heuristic is adopted that prioritizes items according to their value-to-weight ratio. ## D DETAILED METHODOLOGY 

D.1 PRS P ROMPT 

PRS uses a shared prompt library across all problem settings. It contains two system prompts that regulate the behavior of the LLM as an idea generator and as a code generator, respectively, as well as seven workflow prompt templates. These templates are used for: (i) describing the seed heuristic as a natural-language idea, (ii) generating initial heuristic ideas, (iii) applying the Expansion operator, (iv) applying the Compression operator, (v) applying the Steering operator, (vi) realizing an initial idea as executable code, and (vii) rewriting a parent implementation to realize a target idea. Besides those common prompts, each problem setting contains two types of problem-specific prompts: function contract which dictates the format (including the names and types of inputs and outputs) and robustness constraints of designed heuristics, and a task description prompt (i.e., problem description). In this section, we describe their meaning and prompt engineering in detail, where we highlight the uniqueness of the prompt design in red colors. For problem-specific prompt components, we take the problem setting Knapsack Problem in step-by-step constructive framework as an example. • Task Description and Function Contract: While the seed heuristics are only used once in the heuristic pool initialization prompt, the task description and function contract are employed not only during initialization, but also throughout the perturbation and rewrite stages of the evolutionary process. The robustness constraints in function contract do not add inductive bias that explicitly improves the performance of heuristics. Instead, they ensure the validity and consistency of the designed heuristics (e.g., the return must be of the same shape as the distance matrix). F.1.1 DIVERSITY ANALYSIS 

Diversity in evolutionary search provides valuable insights into the exploration strength and the convergence performance of AHD algorithms (Pires et al., 2014). A substantial body of optimization research has investigated the impact of estimating and maintaining diversity within the evolutionary search (Pires et al., 2019; Wang & Chen, 2012; Dat et al., 2025). The evolution curves alone in Section 5 are not sufficient to quantify the diversity within the heuristic search process of our PRS framework. Therefore, we adopt Cumulative Diversity Index (CDI) to quantify diversity, which is the same metric used in previous work, HSEvo (Dat et al., 2025). In the context of heuristic search, CDI reflects how widely heuristic candidates are dispersed in the search space, serving as an implicit signal of system energy, or diversity (Jost, 2006). F.1.2 IDEA -S PACE DIVERSITY ANALYSIS 

CDI measures diversity in the learned representation space of realized heuristic implementations, while mean nearest-neighbor distance (MNND) measures diversity in the semantic idea space before code realization. This distinction is important for PRS because its directional perturba-tion primitives operate directly on natural language heuristic ideas. Distance-based criteria have long been used to characterize dispersion and space-filling properties in multidimensional spaces (Johnson et al., 1990; Morris & Mitchell, 1995), while mean nearest-neighbor distance provides a direct measure of local packing and redundancy (Layman et al., 2007). F.5 FLOW SHOP SCHEDULING PROBLEM UNDER GUIDED LOCAL SEARCH FRAMEWORK 

We adopt the same guided local search (GLS) framework equipped with two neighborhood operators, namely Swap and Relocate as EoH. Within this framework, PRS is employed to automatically design a heuristic strategy that jointly governs the update of the execution-time matrix and the selection of jobs to be perturbed during the search process. The heuristics generated by PRS are compared against a diverse set of non-AHD methods, including both classical heuristics and learning-based approaches. Specifically, we consider the following competitors: (i) GUPTA (Gupta, 1971), a classical heuristic for the flow-shop scheduling problem (FSSP); (ii) CDS (Campbell et al., 1970), another well-established heuristic for FSSP; (iii) NEH (Nawaz et al., 1983), which is widely regarded as an effective heuristic for flow-shop scheduling; (iv) NEHFF (Fernandez-Viagas & Framinan, 2014), an enhanced variant of NEH; (v) PFSPNet (Pan et al., 2021), a recently proposed deep learning-based solver for flow-shop scheduling; (vi) Local Search (LS) , which applies the same neighborhood operators as PRS without heuristic adaptation; (vii) ILS1 (Stützle, 1998), a classical iterated local search method for FSSP; and (viii) ILS2 , which follows the same overall framework as our method but relies on manually designed heuristic rules. We take EoH as the LLM-based AHD baseline. All methods are evaluated on the widely used Taillard (Taillard, 1993) benchmark instances. The number of jobs ranges from 50 to 200, while the number of machines varies between 5 and 20. Each reported value is the average relative gap over 10 instances in the corresponding test set. As shown in Table 11, PRS achieves lower gaps across Taillard instances than EoH in most test sets (e.g., 0.27% vs 0.62% on j100_m20), outperforming it on average while also demonstrating superior performance against non-AHD baselines, including NEH-based heuristics and iterated local search methods. Runtime Cost and efficiency. In terms of wall-clock time, PRS is comparable to lightweight evolutionary methods and significantly faster than search-heavy approaches such as MCTS-AHD across evaluated settings, as shown in Table 17. The total computational cost is primarily governed by the maximum number of evaluations T and the per-heuristic evaluation time. While T is consistent for all LLM-based AHD methods, the per-heuristic evaluation time of PRS remains under control, leading to a competitive total runtime among LLM-based AHD methods. This shows that PRS stands as a practical, cost-efficient AHD framework with strong exploration capability. 

Runtime factors. The overall runtime is primarily dominated by heuristic evaluation rather than LLM inference, and thus scales with the complexity of the heuristics rather than the branching search structure. Heuristics with expensive operations, nested loops, or inefficient data structures can substantially increase per-evaluation runtime, even under the same evaluation budget. As discussed in Appendix F.4, PRS tends to discover heuristics with favorable runtime profiles, particularly on larger problem scales, as evidenced by the Pareto analysis of solution quality and runtime in Fig. 7. 

Search granularity. PRS allocates its LLM budget through bounded BFS expansion. For each selected parent, one idea operator produces a target idea, which is then realized through multiple code rewrites with a fixed beam size. This organization concentrates several candidate realizations around a single idea-level proposal, rather than treating every LLM call as an independent search iteration. As shown in Table 17, this design yields substantially fewer total LLM queries than MCTS-AHD, while retaining a competitive end-to-end runtime. 42 Preprint

## J LIMITATIONS 

A current limitation of PRS is that its adaptive exploration controller still relies on predefined scheduling rules and stagnation thresholds. Although the UCB-based operator selection adapts to recent search outcomes, the overall exploration schedule and restart behavior are manually specified, which may limit the flexibility of search control across different tasks and search stages. Another limitation is the cost of heuristic evaluation. PRS currently realizes each perturbed heuristic idea into executable code before evaluating its performance, even though not every proposed idea is necessarily worth implementation and expensive downstream evaluation. This issue can become particularly important when heuristic evaluation involves large problem instances or computationally costly optimization environments. In future work, we aim to address these limitations by (1) developing more automatic and LLM-driven control mechanisms, where operator selection, exploration scheduling, and stagnation response are determined autonomously by the LLM agent rather than through predefined rules, and (2) introducing surrogate models, such as learned world models, to estimate the potential utility of perturbed heuristic ideas before realization. Such models could prioritize promising ideas and filter less useful ones, reducing unnecessary implementation and evaluation costs and improving the overall efficiency of heuristic search.
