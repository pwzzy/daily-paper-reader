Abstract 

Large language models (LLMs) can translate natural-language optimization problems into solver programs, but reliably producing cor-rect formulations and implementations remains challenging. Existing approaches improve opti-mization modeling through agentic workflows or task-specific post-training. This raises a practical question: can a lightweight harness using a single model role achieve competi-tive performance without additional training? We introduce O PTI CYCLE and O PTI STATE ,two lightweight inference harnesses for opti-mization modeling, and evaluate them on nine benchmarks comprising 2,634 problems using 11 Qwen variants spanning 0.6B–27B param-eters. Our results show that execution-guided revision consistently improves macro accuracy over direct generation for all evaluated models, with gains of up to 49.46 percentage. Despite the simplicity, both harnesses outperform six adapted agentic workflows under same model comparisons and four evaluated post-trained op-timization models at comparable model sizes without additional training. Explicit formula-tion state provides further but model-dependent improvements, suggesting that additional struc-ture is not universally beneficial. 

1 Introduction 

Operations research (OR) provides a principled foundation for decision-making in resource-constrained settings, with optimization models supporting applications such as supply-chain management and transportation (Dantzig, 1963; Hillier et al., 1995). In practice, an optimiza-tion problem often begins as a natural-language description of the decision context, available resources, and desired outcomes. Translating this description into a correct optimization model re-quires identifying decision variables, an objective,

and constraints while preserving the semantics of the original problem (Ramamonjison et al., 2023). Natural-language optimization modeling therefore connects language understanding, mathematical formulation, and executable problem solving. Large language models (LLMs) offer a way to automate this translation from problem descrip-tions to executable optimization models. Existing approaches pursue two complementary directions. One organizes inference through specialized agents or stages for formulation, coding, and debugging (Xiao et al., 2024; AhmadiTeshnizi et al., 2024). The other treats it as a reasoning task and develops domain-specific capabilities through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement learning (RL) (Huang et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025). The two directions differ in whether they modify the inference workflow or the model parameters. Agentic post-training further connects model capabilities with the systems surrounding them: models can be trained to use tools and interact with environments (Kimi Team, 2025; GLM-4.5 Team, 2025), but the opportunities to exercise these capabilities depend on the inference procedure. The same frozen LLM can act as a direct gen-erator or repeatedly execute and revise a solver program. An inference harness is the layer that organizes an LLM’s tools, context, state, and con-trol flow (Meng et al., 2026). Comparisons across optimization systems are difficult to interpret when backbones, benchmarks, inference procedures, and grading protocols all differ. This motivates our central question: how do different harnesses shape the optimization-modeling performance of a frozen LLM, and when does explicit formulation state add value? 

In this paper, we study this question through a unified harness view of optimization modeling (Figure 1). 2 Related Work 

2.1 LLMs for Optimization Modeling 

Recent work on LLM-based optimization modeling broadly follows two methodological directions: improving inference-time modeling procedures and adapting model parameters specifically for optimization tasks. 

Agent Systems for OR. One direction improves optimization modeling through task-specific 2prompting and structured inference workflows. Existing systems organize formulation, coding, execution and debugging into coordinated stages or specialized roles (Deng et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2024; AhmadiTeshnizi et al., 2024; Monteiro et al., 2026). Beyond stage decomposition, recent approaches explore formulation search, execution-aware verification, iterative repair, and the reuse of correction experiences (Astorga et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025; Song et al., 2026; Xie et al., 2026). These methods improve the inference process without necessarily changing the underlying model. Our work focuses on this setting, studying OR-oriented harnesses that support iterative modeling within a single agent role while holding model weights fixed. 

Reasoning Models for Optimization Modeling. 

A complementary direction develops optimization-specific reasoning through parameter adaptation. Data synthesis and SFT provide supervision for formulation and code generation (Huang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025), while solver-grounded RL and process supervision further develop optimization reasoning (Chen et al., 2025; Xiao et al., 2026; Tang et al., 2026; Guan et al., 2026). Related approaches extend parameter adaptation to test time (Ding et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026b). Our study keeps model weights fixed. In particular, O PTI STATE 

draws on LLMOPT’s five-element formulation (Jiang et al., 2025) as a revisable inference-time state rather than training supervision. 

Benchmarks for Optimization Modeling. 

Optimization-modeling benchmarks have progressively expanded in scope and difficulty. NL4Opt focuses on natural-language linear programming problems (Ramamonjison et al., 2023), while NLP4LP covers both linear and mixed-integer programming (AhmadiTeshnizi et al., 2024). MAMO and ComplexOR broaden the range of modeling tasks and problem structures (Huang et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2024). IndustryOR, OptiBench, and OptMATH further extend the evaluation to more diverse domains, formulations, and levels of complexity (Huang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2025). We use existing optimization-modeling tasks as a controlled testbed for comparing inference-time harness designs. 

2.2 Agentic Language Models and Harnesses 

LLM-based agents have developed through both externally organized workflows and improvements in model capabilities. Approaches such as AutoGen and MetaGPT coordinate specialized roles, while ReAct and Reflexion connect reasoning with actions and feedback without updating model weights (Wu et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2023; Shinn et al., 2023). More recently, agentic RL incorporates tool use and environment interaction into model post-training (Kimi Team, 2025; GLM-4.5 Team, 2025). Evaluating the resulting capabilities calls for both task-level benchmarks and an explicit account of the harness used at inference time. 

Agentic Benchmarks. General-purpose evalua-tions assess software maintenance, terminal tasks, and program reconstruction, as exemplified by SWE-bench, Terminal-Bench, and ProgramBench (Jimenez et al., 2024; Merrill et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026). Within OR, recent benchmarks evaluate end-to-end workflows and solver-guided model repair (Li et al., 2026a; Ao et al., 2026). Re-lated combinatorial optimization benchmarks focus on algorithm and heuristic design (Sun et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026a). We use existing optimization modeling tasks to investigate how harnesses affect frozen LLMs under a shared evaluation protocol. 

Inference Harnesses. A harness organizes the tools, context, state, and control flow surrounding an LLM (Meng et al., 2026). Coding-agent systems such as Claude Code, Codex CLI, and OpenCode provide implementations of this inference-time layer, whereas Harbor provides shared evaluation interfaces for running agents across tasks and environments (Anthropic, 2026; OpenAI, 2026; OpenCode, 2026; Harbor, 2026). Recent research examines how harness choices affect measured performance and how harnesses can be composed and adapted (Yao et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026b). We investigate this design space for optimization modeling through O PTI CYCLE and O PTI STATE ,treating Direct Generation as the single-step special case. 

3 A Unified Harness View 

We organize optimization modeling around a frozen language model and a harness that controls its execution environment, interaction history, and persistent state. Within this view, 3Direct Generation is the single-step setting, OPTI CYCLE introduces execution-guided revision, and O PTI STATE additionally exposes a revisable formulation (Figure 1). Both of our harnesses use one model role and keep its weights fixed throughout inference. 

3.1 Task and Harness Interface 

Given a natural-language problem q and its numerical data, the model must infer decision variables and their domains, the objective, and the constraints, then implement and solve the formulation as a solver program. The submitted answer is an optimal objective value or astatus in {infeasible , unbounded }. Missing or unparseable submissions count as failures. The harness separates the model’s proposed computation from its execution. The model produces code, the environment runs it, and the controller determines what feedback and state are available for the next call and when to stop. Direct Generation makes one model call to produce a com-plete solver program and executes it once. It has no opportunity to revise the program after observing its behavior. Our interactive harnesses maintain execution state across calls and provide summaries of recent actions and observations, allowing the same model to revise its work. 

3.2 Iterative Refinement through Execution Feedback 

OPTI CYCLE organizes optimization modeling as a sequence of code generation, execution, observation, and revision. The model can build a solver program incrementally, inspect its behavior, and choose its next operation from the execution results. Each trajectory runs in a persistent Python interpreter, so imported modules, variables, and solver objects remain available across turns. This continuity lets the model resume from its current work: it can repair an indexing error, modify a constraint, or rerun a solver using objects it has already created. The harness separates persistent execution state from the context presented to the model. The interpreter retains live objects, workspace files store intermediate artifacts, and the prompt combines the original problem with a bounded summary of recent actions and execution feedback. These summaries record the purpose of each action and the resulting output or exception. Together with optimization-specific modeling instructions and recovery hints, they guide the next code opera-tion while keeping the interaction context compact. Each action at specifies a purpose and code ct.The loop proceeds as

3.3 Explicit Formulation as Revisable State 

OPTI STATE extends this design by making the model’s interpretation of the optimization problem an explicit part of the workspace. A separate formulation records the decisions that guide implementation: which entities are indexed, which quantities are known, what can be chosen and how the objective and constraints relate to these quantities. Keeping these decisions together gives the model a structured reference as it develops the solver program. Before code execution begins, an initial model call constructs a five-element formulation, following LLMOPT (Jiang et al., 2025): 

z0 = ( zset , z param , z var , z obj , z con ). (2) The components describe sets and indices, known parameters, decision variables and their domains, the objective, and constraints. The formulation is saved in the workspace and supplied as a context for code generation. The model translates this representation into solver operations within the same persistent execution loop as O PTI CYCLE .The formulation remains editable throughout the trajectory. The model can inspect the stored representation, update its variable definitions or constraints, and revise the corresponding solver code. For example, a change in how a quantity is indexed can be recorded in the formulation and carried through to variable creation and constraint construction. This makes formulation part of the ongoing modeling process, extending the agent’s working state beyond Python objects and execution history. Adding explicit formulation state produces selective gains (Figure 2, left). The largest improvements occur on Qwen3.5-4B and

5 Analysis 

5.1 Where Interaction Helps Most 

Our results suggest that a simple execution-and-revision harness is most useful when tasks challenge direct generation but remain within the model’s capabilities. ComplexOR illustrates this pattern: O PTI CYCLE substantially improves over Direct Generation on both shared backbones. Gains are smaller on MAMO-easy for Qwen3-4B 

and Qwen3-8B , where direct generation accuracy is already high. Interaction also offers little benefit for some difficult model–task pairs, such as 

Qwen3-8B on MAMO-complex (Tables 5 and 6). These findings are consistent with a harness helping models make better use of their existing capabilities, with limited headroom on easier tasks and persistent capability constraints on harder ones. 5.2 Formulation State Helps Some Models and Tasks 

Explicit formulation state provides additional gains beyond executable interaction, but its value depends on the backbone and task. For 

Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3.5-4B , OPTI STATE 

improves over OPTI CYCLE on all nine benchmarks, indicating broad benefits for these two models (Tables 5 and 6). For Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B , the effects are mixed. Both models improve on NL4Opt and ICML Competition but lose accuracy on OptMath and ComplexOR. The small changes in macro accuracy therefore mask opposing effects across individual benchmarks. These results suggest that the value of explicit formulation state should be assessed for each model and task, rather than assumed to improve the execution loop uniformly.

6 Conclusion and Future Work 

We studied optimization modeling as an executable inference task for frozen language models. Across nine benchmarks and 11 Qwen variants, a lightweight execution-and-repair harness substantially improves performance over direct generation for models above 1B parameters and outperforms the evaluated adapted architectures with Qwen3-4B and Qwen3.5-9B .Explicit formulation state provides additional, model-dependent gains. The same workflow also improves accuracy on Phi, Magistral, and GPT-OSS. 

Future work. An important direction is a

self-evolving harness that improves its inference workflow across tasks while keeping the underlying model frozen. Rather than revis-ing only a task’s code or formulation, such a framework could use historical execution trajectories to propose changes to prompts, 8feedback processing, state representations, and repair-budget allocation. Candidate harnesses could be evaluated on a separate development set and retained when they improve accuracy under explicit cost constraints. 

Limitations 

While our harnesses improve optimization-modeling performance across the evaluated bench-marks, the experiments compare complete work-flows with method-specific inference budgets. In particular, O PTI STATE adds both an initial formu-lation call and a revisable representation to O PTI -CYCLE . We report recorded token consumption for the Qwen3.5-9B comparison; matched-budget ab-lations, trajectory-level repair analysis, and latency measurements can further clarify the contributions and costs of individual components. We have primarily evaluated 11 Qwen vari-ants, with additional experiments on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, Phi-4-reasoning, Magistral-Small-2509, and GPT-OSS-20B. Configuration re-visions differ across model generations, so these results characterize the evaluated model–harness settings. The reported accuracies are point esti-mates without uncertainty intervals; broader model coverage and uncertainty quantification would fur-ther strengthen the empirical findings. Finally, our evaluation uses objective-value or status agreement as a common outcome-level mea-sure across benchmarks. Extending verification to decision-vector feasibility, constraint satisfac-tion, and formulation equivalence would provide a more comprehensive assessment of modeling cor-rectness. 

Ethical Considerations 

This work studies the use of language models for automated optimization modeling. Incorrect for-mulations or solver implementations may produce misleading solutions, especially in consequential decision-making settings. Our evaluation focuses on benchmark accuracy and does not establish reli-ability for real-world deployment. Human verifica-tion remains important before applying generated optimization models. Additionally, repeated model inference and solver execution incur computational costs.

A.3 Execution and Grading 

Generated programs execute in Python with SciPy, PuLP, and PySCIPOpt. The runner uses host sub-processes within temporary task directories; hidden grader files are not copied into these directories. Baseline inference used eight independent vLLM endpoints. Structured JSON, routing, and compila-tion stages may use direct decoding, while stages involving semantic analysis, search, or traceback debugging retain reasoning, according to each har-ness’s configuration. For each benchmark, avg@ k is the percentage of correct trajectories among all planned samples over all problems. Trajectories with missing scores count as incorrect and remain in the denominator. It is an estimate of average single-trajectory accu-racy, not the probability that at least one of k sam-ples succeeds. Macro accuracy is the unweighted mean of these benchmark accuracies. The grader compares numerical objective values using abso-lute tolerance 10 −2 and relative tolerance 10 −4,or checks agreement on infeasible /unbounded
