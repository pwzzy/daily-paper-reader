Abstract 

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being explored to automate operations research modeling, reduce modeling time and lower the expertise barrier to optimization based decision sup-port. However, this process often introduces inconsistencies between generated artifacts and the original problem, which we refer to as optimization modeling hallucinations. Despite progress in general-purpose hallucination mitigation, optimization modeling poses a distinct repair challenge: restoring fidelity to the original problem through coordinated model–code updates. We introduce OPT-Kraken, a training-free hallucination repair framework. To the best of our knowledge, it is the first post-hoc framework for re-pairing hallucinations in LLM-based optimization modeling artifacts. It extracts problem requirements and instance data, localizes errors by checking problem–model fidelity, model–code consistency, and data grounding, and selects repair granularity by the affected scope. For local repair, category rules and instance evidence are compiled into contracts specifying permitted edits, expected effects, and protected content. Experiments on established optimization modeling benchmarks show positive gains across au-tomatic modeling frameworks and fine-tuned models. On zero-shot artifacts from multiple base models, OPT-Kraken effectively repairs optimization modeling hallucinations while preserving initially correct artifacts. These results demonstrate effective and controlled repair for LLM-based optimization model-ing artifacts. 

## 1 Introduction 

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being explored to translate natural language operations research (OR) problems into mathematical models M and solver code S. This workflow can lower the expertise barrier and reduce the modeling time required for decision support in manufacturing, logistics, and resource allocation. However, M and S generated by LLM often fail to remain faithful to the original problem, potentially misleading downstream decisions. We define optimization modeling hallucinations 

as errors in LLM-generated modeling artifacts that make them inconsistent with the original optimization problem. These include unsupported additions, omitted requirements, incorrect mathematical formulations, model–code inconsistencies, data-binding faults, and result-extraction errors, among other failures. General hallucination research provides foundations for evidence checking, consistency estimation, and feedback-driven revision. However, unlike question-answering tasks whose primary outputs are general nat-ural language responses, optimization modeling requires translating problem requirements into interrelated mathematical models and solver code. Therefore, repairing optimization modeling hallucinations requires locating errors using evidence from the original problem and defining the scope of coordinated model–code updates. These requirements motivate treating hallucination repair as a standalone post-hoc task on optimization modeling artifacts. Rather than simply generating another model and code, the task is to identify errors in existing artifacts using evidence from the original problem, perform necessary repairs, and use validation to decide whether to accept the changes. Figure 1 illustrates a shared modeling hallucination and its localized repair: both the mathematical model and solver code use 5B ≤ 2A, whereas the original problem requires 

5B ≥ 2A. The repair corrects the inequality direction in both artifacts while leaving variable domains, objective coefficients, and other constraints unchanged. We study hallucination repair through the joint lens of error correction and content preservation. In-correct outputs deemed hallucination-free lose repair opportunities; false alarms can corrupt correct outputs through unnecessary edits. Repair must correct errors while preserving valid content. We therefore use three metrics: Rescue , Regression , and Error Coverage . Rescue is the share of initially incorrect outputs corrected, Regression is the share of initially correct outputs made incorrect, and Error Coverage is the share of initially incorrect outputs retained as repair opportunities by the screening stage. Together, they capture gains, outcome-level harm, and coverage of opportunities. OPT-Kraken provides plug-and-play post-processing of a problem P , model M0, and code S0, without retraining or changing the upstream generator. It extracts requirements O and instance data D from problem 

P , localizes errors through directional fidelity checks, and selects the repair scope. Contracts bind category rules to case evidence; commit gates protect valid content. To the best of our knowledge, OPT-Kraken is the first post-hoc framework for repairing hallucinations in LLM-generated optimization-modeling artifacts, targeting problem–model–code fidelity while controlling repair scope and regression. Our contributions can be summarized as follows: 

(1) Post-hoc formulation of hallucination repair for LLM-based optimization modeling: We formulate hallucination repair for LLM-based optimization modeling as a post-hoc transformation of frozen problem– model–code artifacts. Unlike generation-time correction, the task requires correcting unfaithful content while preserving valid content. We evaluate repair effectiveness, preservation, and coverage of repair op-portunities using Rescue, Regression, and Error Coverage, defined respectively as the shares of initially 2incorrect outputs corrected, initially correct outputs made incorrect, and initially incorrect outputs flagged by screening for examination or potential repair. 

(2) Evidence-guided and scope-controlled hallucination repair framework: We introduce OPT-Kraken, a generic training-free framework that operates as a post-processor for different upstream generators. It (a) diagnoses artifacts through source-grounded checks of problem–model fidelity, model–code consistency, and data grounding; (b) selects the minimum sufficient repair scope based on error location and dependency scope; and (c) compiles category-level repair knowledge and instance-specific evidence into executable contracts for reliable local repair, followed by artifact-level auditing and safe commit. 

(3) Consistent improvements across diverse optimization-modeling generators: We evaluate six bench-marks, four generation pipelines, four fine-tuned generators, and three repair backbones. Accuracy improves by 6.3–20.1 percentage points across the four generation pipelines. Three complementary ablations exam-ine the roles of dual-evidence detection, evidence-guided repair granularity, and contract-constrained local repair. 

## 2 Related Work 

General LLM hallucination detection and mitigation. Hallucination research studies factual errors and deviations from source content (Ji et al., 2023). FActScore assesses source support for atomic claims (Min et al., 2023), while SelfCheckGPT uses sampling-based consistency for detection (Manakul et al., 2023). RARR revises unsupported content while retaining the original text where possible (Gao et al., 2023), and CRITIC uses tool feedback for correction (Gou et al., 2024). However, claim-level grounding and output consistency alone do not establish complete requirement coverage or fidelity across mathematical models and solver code. OPT-Kraken addresses these issues through cross-artifact checks grounded in problem requirements and instance data. 

Generation and training for LLM-based optimization modeling. Chain-of-Experts, OptiMUS, ORMind, and OptiTree organize generation through expert collaboration, feedback, counterfactual reasoning, or tree search; NEMO adds optimizer–simulator validation (Xiao et al., 2024; Ahmaditeshnizi et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025; Song et al., 2026). ORLM, LLMOPT, ReSocratic, and OptMATH improve training through customizable instructions, multi-element modeling, inverse construction, and bidirectional synthesis (Huang et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025; Lu et al., 2025). These designs center on generating correct models and code, but do not by themselves establish that the resulting artifacts are faithful to the original problem. Reliable post-processing repair is therefore needed to assess problem fidelity and correct affected artifacts while preserving valid content. 

Verification and correction for LLM-based optimization modeling. SIRL improves modeling through solver-informed learning, while ORLoopBench studies solver-in-the-loop diagnosis and repair (Chen et al., 2025; Ao et al., 2026). StepORLM combines solver outcomes with process supervision (Zhou et al., 2026); SAC-Opt uses semantic anchors for code correction (Zhang et al., 2026); and Opt-Verifier combines struc-tural and solution verification (Liu et al., 2026). Existing work primarily treats modeling errors as failures to be avoided during generation or corrected within refinement workflows, while scope-aware repair that preserves correct content remains underexplored. In contrast, we study repair as a post-hoc transforma-tion of frozen artifacts, where unnecessary edits to initially correct model–code pairs are themselves fail-ures. ## 3 Methodology 

3.1 Problem Formulation and Available Information 

Given a natural-language optimization problem P , an upstream generator produces a mathematical model 

M0 and solver code S0. We check whether the model and code are faithful to P . Unsupported additions, omitted requirements, incorrect formulations, model–code inconsistencies, data-binding faults, and result-extraction errors count as hallucinations. Equivalent forms remain valid. OPT-Kraken takes problem P and the upstream generator’s artifacts M0 and S0 as input, and returns 

(M ∗, S ∗). It aims to rescue incorrect artifacts while preserving correct content. Its goal can be summarized as follows: after processing by OPT-Kraken, the model is faithful to P , the code faithfully implements the model, and both use the instance data correctly. Domain requirements come only from P . Detection reads fixed P/M 0/S 0 and evidence from P . It excludes upstream solve status, error messages, objective values, and reference answers. Execution checks follow candidate generation; references are used only for offline evaluation. Figure 2 shows the OPT-Kraken workflow: directional checks produce grounded findings; affected scope and evidence determine repair granularity; contracts bound local edits. All candidates undergo artifact auditing before commit; C LEAN inputs are retained. 

3.2 Dual Evidence and Directional Fidelity Checks

## 4 Experiments 

4.1 Setup and Evaluation Metrics 

Benchmarks and paired evaluation. We evaluate on MAMO Complex, IndustryOR, ComplexOR, Opt-MATH, NLP4LP, and BWOR, covering compound constraints, industrial applications, and long-form data. Before-and-after comparisons use fixed P/M 0/S 0 from each upstream method. We include usable M/S 

and follow the established reference-conflict exclusions. Zero-shot denotes a pipeline that generates a model and then code, without worked examples. The generation frameworks are ORMind, OptiMUS-0.3, and SAC-Opt. The fine-tuned generators are ORLM-LLaMA-3-8B, SIRL-Qwen2.5-7B-Gurobi, LLMOPT-Qwen2.5-14B, and OptMATH-Qwen2.5-7B. DeepSeek V4 Flash repairs both groups; it, Qwen3.8-27B, and GPT-5.6 Sol also repair self-generated zero-shot out-puts. Gurobi is the solver. 

Metrics. Accuracy checks objective values or required solve status, with absolute and relative tolerances of 10 −2 and 10 −4. Let E0 and C0 denote outputs scored incorrect and correct, respectively, before repair. Rescue is the fraction of E0 made correct, Regression is the fraction of C0 made incorrect, and Error Cover-age is the fraction of E0 assigned a non-C LEAN screening outcome and thus retained for further examination or potential repair. Repair eligibility for a non-C LEAN output depends on evidence and routing. Objective/s-tatus labels score outcomes, not the broader hallucination space of requirement, formulation, model–code, data-binding, and result-extraction errors; thus, outcome correctness need not imply complete modeling-artifact fidelity (Appendix C.1 quantifies this disagreement). Overall results aggregate paired instances per upstream. Cross-upstream means equally weight the eight methods in Tables 2 and 3; all values are in %.

This variation is consistent with different affected scopes and coupling: a fixed preference for local repair does not represent the successful paths of all upstreams. It supports treating evidence-guided granularity selection as a mechanism for accommodating heterogeneous errors. These are successful-path shares, not routing accuracy or a local-only counterfactual; ablation B below provides complementary evidence for the hierarchy together with its branch evidence and permissions.

## 5 Conclusion 

OPT-Kraken combines dual-evidence diagnosis, evidence-guided granularity selection, and contract-constrained repair with artifact-level auditing and safe commit. By treating hallucination repair as an independent post-hoc transformation of frozen problem–model–code artifacts, OPT-Kraken makes error correction and con-tent preservation joint requirements of optimization-modeling repair. Across six benchmarks, OPT-Kraken improves accuracy and Rescue while maintaining broad Error Coverage and low Regression, moving LLM-based optimization modeling beyond a sole focus on initial success toward evidence-grounded hallucination screening and risk-controlled artifact repair. 11 Reproducibility Statement

## C Detailed Experimental Results 

Initial and Final report Accuracy before and after repair. Let E0 and C0 denote outputs scored incorrect and correct, respectively, before repair. Rescue is the fraction of E0 made correct, Error Coverage is the fraction of E0 assigned a non-C LEAN screening outcome, and Regression is the fraction of C0 made incorrect. These outcome labels follow objective/status scoring; the detector additionally examines formulation, model–code consistency, data-binding, and result-extraction errors. Metric entries in the detailed result tables are per-centages. For DeepSeek V4 Flash Zero-shot, among the 442 initially incorrect outputs, 245 (55.4%) were assigned E RROR , 171 (38.7%) were assigned U NRESOLVED , and 26 (5.9%) were assigned C LEAN . The resulting non-C LEAN Error Coverage is 94.1%. 

Metric interpretation. Regression is not a detector false-positive rate: a correct input may receive a non-C LEAN outcome without Regression if no candidate passes audit and the original is retained. Error Coverage measures coverage of potential repair opportunities, whereas low Regression indicates outcome-level preservation of initially correct outputs. Neither metric measures detection precision or specificity or establishes calibrated binary hallucination detection. 

C.1 Outcome-Level Correctness and Modeling-Artifact Fidelity 

Following the established benchmark protocols, we evaluate all methods using objective values or the solve status required by each problem to ensure deterministic, reproducible, and consistent evaluation across meth-ods. This criterion is not strictly equivalent to complete fidelity of the modeling artifacts to the original prob-lem: an incorrect modeling element may, for example, be inactive at the optimum of the current instance and leave the reported objective unchanged. To quantify this discrepancy, we manually reviewed the initial artifacts for all 359 numerically correct DeepSeek V4 Flash Zero-shot results. Each artifact was inspected for substantive inconsistencies between the original problem and the generated model–code artifacts. We found 4 artifacts containing optimization-modeling hallucinations that outcome-level evaluation did not expose: the generated artifacts were not fully faithful to the problem, yet the numerical result was coincidentally correct. Paired pre- and post-repair source fidelity. We further examine whether the outcome-level improve-ments are accompanied by improvements in the fidelity of the generated artifacts to the source problem. We consider the 339 initially outcome-incorrect DeepSeek V4 Flash Zero-shot artifacts selected for evidence-controlled follow-up under the screening policy. For each instance, both the initial artifact (P, M 0, S 0) and the final retained artifact (P, M ∗, S ∗) are manually reviewed. Pre- and post-repair artifacts are anonymized and randomly shuffled before review, so annotators are unaware of repair status. The review is conducted by researchers with expertise in operations research, and boundary cases are resolved through adjudication. Each artifact is assigned one of three source-fidelity labels: faithful (F) , unfaithful (U) , or indetermi-nate (I) . An artifact is labeled faithful when no substantive inconsistency or unsupported modeling assump-tion is found relative to the source problem. It is labeled unfaithful when such a discrepancy is present. A case is labeled indeterminate when the source problem is internally inconsistent or does not uniquely determine a material modeling convention, so fidelity cannot be resolved without introducing an external assumption. Source-fidelity labels are assigned without using the benchmark reference objective or solve-status la-bel. Consequently, source fidelity and outcome correctness need not coincide: a source-faithful artifact may still receive an outcome-incorrect label because of benchmark/reference disagreements, evaluator or inter-face mismatches, or execution/numerical failures, while outcome correctness may mask modeling errors as illustrated in Table 7. Cases whose fidelity cannot be resolved directly from the source text are adjudicated under the same source-fidelity criterion. Taxonomy and RepairCard construction. The expert process was used to develop the hallucination taxonomy and RepairCards, not to assign correctness labels to the formal evaluation outputs. Optimization-modeling experts, including two doctoral researchers, first reviewed naturally generated problem–model– code artifacts from multiple development benchmarks and generation models. They drafted categories around objectives, decision variables, constraints, data mapping, code implementation, and result extraction. Multiple LLMs, including DeepSeek, served as discovery aids for surfacing uncovered patterns, consolidat-ing near-synonymous categories, and identifying boundary cases. We also created a small set of controlled elicitation cases by changing directions, indices, ratios, units, or constraint conditions to induce rare failure modes and stress-test coverage. The experts jointly refined category boundaries, inclusion and exclusion rules, positive and negative examples, and corresponding card eligibility conditions until reaching consen-sus. This was an iterative design process rather than independent annotation of a fixed evaluation cohort. Controlled cases were used only for taxonomy and card development; they are excluded from the reported hallucination frequencies and from all Rescue and Regression results. 

Development–evaluation separation and freeze point. Every instance used to design the taxonomy, Re-pairCards, or routing rules came from development benchmarks that are disjoint from the six benchmarks used for formal evaluation.
